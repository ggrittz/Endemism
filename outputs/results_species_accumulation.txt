#### MAIN METHODS AND RESULTS FROM THE EVALUATION OF THE SPECIES ACCUMULATION CURVE (from code: 12_species_accumulation.r) ####

We obtained from the WCVP backbone (version X) the date of first publication for each species. 
This date corresponded to the oldest date of publication when accepted names contained basionyms or synonyms published prior to the accepted name.

We then calculated the cumulative number of known species each year until 2022.


The non-linear Generalized Ricker model (i.e. species ~ b * (years/a * exp(1 - years/a))^alpha), was the one that by far presented the vest fit to the species accumulation curve.

This hump-shaped model has a parameter b which estimates the peak of the curve, which in our context is a good estimate for the maximum number of species that will be described in our study area in the future.

Based on the fitted model, we obtained the predicted y-value at the peak (i.e the estimated number of species) and the x-value at the peak (the estimated year when all species will be described).
Using the function predictNLS() of the package propagate (Spiess, A. (2025). propagate: Propagation of Uncertainty. R package version 1.0-7, <https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=propagate>.), we obtained the 95% prediction intervals based on uncertainty propagation.

The earliest species in our study area were described in 1753. The rate of species described each year increased until the early 20s of the last century, when the rate started to decrease. 

However, we estimated that there still are 17384 tree species to be discovered in tropical America, a feat that will result in a total of 42891 tree species [95% CI: 40166-46243] and that will not be achieved before the year of 2347 [95% CI: 2311-2390], given the historical rate of species discovery.
